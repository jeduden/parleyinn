# How dots, Amp orbs and Buzz pitch: length and structure

Read on 4 October 2026 from each product's own launch pages, to set
the length and shape of ParleyInn's pitch.

| Product   | Page                                        | Words       | Shape                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------- | ------------------------------------------- | ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| dots      | [Introducing dots][dots], 29 September 2026 | about 1,400 | A bold one-line claim, six paragraphs of benefit and story, then five headed sections (on their own, an extension of you, where you work, in control, specialists) and a get-started close |
| Amp       | [What Are Orbs?][orbs] landing              | about 410   | Six one-line benefits in a carousel, six numbered cards of one to three sentences, seven feature tiles of one sentence; a longer essay below                                               |
| Buzz      | [Introducing Buzz][buzz], 21 July 2026      | about 710   | Release line, the insight ("humans and agents in the same room"), then What Buzz is, Building on Nostr, Why open source, How to get started                                                |
| Buzz      | [buzz.xyz][buzzsite] landing                | about 20    | One line: "Your people, your agents, your project — all in one place."                                                                                                                     |
| ParleyInn | pitch v12, short                            | 300         | Headline, problem paragraph, the screen, then six one-to-two-sentence claims                                                                                                               |

## What each does

- **dots** leads with a claim about the user's time, tells three short
  stories of work inside OpenAI and one tester's invoice, and saves
  safety for its own section near the end, with named controls
  (custom rules, activity view, auto-review).
- **Amp** never states a problem. It sells by imperative benefits
  ("Send your prompt and close your laptop"), then makes each one
  concrete in a card. Every sentence is short; the page reads in a
  minute.
- **Buzz** argues a belief (work happens when humans and agents share a
  room; that place should be open) and spends a third of its words on
  why the protocol and the licence matter.

## For ParleyInn

- The landing line can be one sentence, as Buzz's is; the pitch below
  it can stay near Amp's length, about 300 to 400 words.
- Amp's imperative one-liners and numbered cards are the shape closest
  to a developer skimming; dots's short stories show the product
  working without describing it.
- None of the three opens with the reader's pain, as ParleyInn does.
