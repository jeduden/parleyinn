The inn, numbered

ParleyInn’s domain model reads like the inventory of a frontier inn, so the picture draws it as written. Robot hands sit at the card table, every seat runs a tab, and the stage at the platform belongs to another inn, enrolled by key.

Pick a name in the key or a number in the picture, and the picture takes you there.

Key to the picture

Your agents, part of your team

  • At your pace.

    Work with your agents and other people live, after the fact or asynchronously: watch runs as they happen, see what waits on you, or come back to a Catch up that links each line to the events behind it and shows every gap.

  • From every angle.

    Understand what is being built while it is being built: the conversation, each changed file beside the events that changed it, and what backs each result, from a bare claim to a check.

  • By reference.

    Link your posts to the exact events you mean, by address, and find any of them again by a word you remember, so people and agents know what you are talking about.

Underneath, every run stays on your machines unless you turn on peers. Your pinned constraints come back word for word after each compaction, or named when the budget leaves them out. Agents recall history only when they ask, each item enveloped with its trust level, and nothing untrusted reaches them unless they ask or you act.

The contract

The invariants are ParleyInn’s contract. Every requirement serves at least one of them, and a change that would break one is a design change, not a bug fix.

  1. I1Nothing is lost.
  2. I2No automatic path from untrusted content to the model.
  3. I3Constraints are never summarized.
  4. I4Each component stays inside one declared network boundary, and only the core builds what reaches the model.
  5. I5Bad data can be removed from circulation without destroying the record.
  6. I6No silent failures.
  7. I7Harness configuration changes only on explicit instruction.
  8. I8Isolation follows the principal.
  9. I9ParleyInn never degrades the agent.
  10. I10Everything derived is rebuildable.

Where it stands

Specified, not built yet. The repository holds:

  • the Software Requirements Specification, the normative source, and the domain model that defines its terms;
  • one Gherkin scenario per requirement under features/, almost all @pending, kept in step with the SRS by a gate test;
  • the parinn core executable, with parinn version and parinn help so far;
  • CI, a nightly fuzz job, an agent review of every pull request and a signed release pipeline, every gate written in Rust;
  • the delivery plans in PLAN.md.

ParleyInn is MIT licensed and specified in the open: the requirements, the domain model and the plans. Report a vulnerability privately, as SECURITY.md says.
